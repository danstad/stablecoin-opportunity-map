# Literature Review: Stablecoin Opportunity Map — Economic Complexity Meets Payment Innovation

**Date:** 2026-04-03
**Librarian-critic score:** 62/100 (Round 1) → Revisions applied

## 1. Research Question

Which country × industry links stand to benefit most from stablecoin adoption? We combine the Hausmann-Hidalgo economic complexity framework (Product Space, ECI, PCI) with payment friction analysis in a structural gravity model, estimating both intensive and extensive margin effects.

---

## 2. Literature Landscape (5 Streams)

### Stream A: Economic Complexity (24 papers found)
**Core:** Hidalgo et al. (2007, Science), Hidalgo & Hausmann (2009, PNAS), Hausmann et al. (2014), Stojkoski & Hidalgo (2025)
**Key extensions:** Bahar et al. (2014, JIE) on neighbor knowledge diffusion, Hartmann et al. (2017, World Development) on complexity and inequality, Balland et al. (2019, 2022) on smart specialization and relatedness
**Financial link:** Ndoya et al. (2024) show financial development increases ECI with threshold effects; Manova (2013) establishes that financial frictions affect trade by sector

### Stream B: Stablecoins & Cross-Border Flows (22 papers found)
**Core:** Auer, Lewrick & Paulick (2025, BIS WP 1265) — gravity model of crypto flows across 184 countries; Reuter (2025, IMF WP) — geographic attribution of stablecoin flows; Cerutti et al. (2024, 2026, IMF) — measurement and macro effects
**Real economy effects:** Copestake et al. (2026) — GENIUS Act event study; Aldasoro et al. (2026) — FX spillovers; Barthelemy et al. (2024, BdF) — stablecoin reserve demand
**Dollarization:** Murakami & Viswanath-Natraj (2025, JIMF) — sovereign risk drives stablecoin adoption in EMs

### Stream C: Payment Infrastructure & Trade Costs (19 papers found)
**Core:** Ferrari Minesso et al. (2026, ECB WP 3202) — payment system links increase trade ~4%; Borchert et al. (2024, CEPR) — losing correspondent banking reduces exports 9.7pp
**Financial frictions:** Manova (2013, REStud), Antras & Foley (2015, JPE), Niepmann & Schmidt-Eisenlohr (2017, JIE), Crozet et al. (2022)
**Currency invoicing:** Gopinath et al. (2020, AER) — dominant currency paradigm
**Tech analog:** Freund & Weinhold (2004, JIE) — internet and trade

### Stream D: Currency Unions & Dollarization (added per critic)
**Core:** Rose (2000) — common currencies roughly triple trade; Glick & Rose (2002) — panel extension; Frankel & Rose (2002, QJE) — endogeneity of OCA criteria
**Dollarization theory:** Alesina & Barro (2002, QJE) — costs/benefits of dollarization
**Relevance:** USD stablecoins are functionally voluntary dollarization — this literature provides THE benchmark for gravity estimates

### Stream E: CBDC Cross-Border Payments (added per critic)
**Core:** Auer, Haene & Holden (2021, BIS) — multi-CBDC arrangements
**Relevance:** CBDCs are the main institutional competitor to stablecoins for cross-border payments

## 3. Frontier Map: What's Done vs. What's Open

### Done:
- Measuring cross-border crypto/stablecoin flows (Auer 2025, Reuter 2025)
- Gravity estimation of aggregate stablecoin flows (Auer 2025)
- Causal effect of payment system interlinking on trade (Ferrari Minesso 2026: ~4%)
- Causal effect of de-risking on exports (Borchert 2024: -9.7pp)
- Common currency trade effects (Rose 2000: ~3x, later refined to ~30-50%)
- Financial frictions × sector vulnerability on trade (Manova 2013)
- Economic complexity measurement and growth prediction (Hidalgo 2007, 2009)

### NOT Done (Our Gaps):
1. **No paper connects stablecoin adoption to product-level trade flows**
2. **No paper integrates economic complexity with payment infrastructure**
3. **No paper models heterogeneous stablecoin effects across product types**
4. **No predictive "opportunity maps" combining complexity + payment frictions**
5. **The dominant-currency-paradigm / stablecoin connection is unexplored**

### Scooping Risk: LOW
Closest competitors (Auer et al. 2025, Ferrari Minesso et al. 2026) study different outcomes and lack product-level complexity dimension.

## 4. Positioning

### Contribution Statement (Draft)
> This paper makes three contributions. First, we integrate the economic complexity framework (Hidalgo & Hausmann, 2009) with a structural gravity model to show that payment frictions disproportionately reduce trade in complex products. Second, we develop a Stablecoin Opportunity Score that identifies country × product pairs where stablecoin adoption would most increase trade. Third, we provide the first systematic mapping of bilateral trade corridors and product categories that stand to gain most from stablecoin-enabled payment infrastructure.

### Key Differentiation
| Competitor | What they do | What we add |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Auer et al. (2025 BIS) | Gravity of crypto flows | Product-level goods trade + complexity |

| Borchert et al. (2024 CEPR) | De-risking → export decline | Stablecoin solution + complexity dimension |
| Manova (2013 REStud) | Credit constraints × sector vulnerability | Payment frictions × product complexity |
| Rose (2000) | Common currency → trade | Voluntary, corridor-specific dollarization via stablecoins |

## 6. Anticipated Referee Concerns

1. **"How does the stablecoin effect compare to the common currency effect?"** → Benchmark against Rose (2000) coefficients; stablecoins should have a smaller effect (they reduce payment costs but don't eliminate exchange rate risk for non-USD pairs)
2. **"Endogeneity of payment frictions"** → IV using de-risking shocks, regulatory events, or historical correspondent banking geography
3. **"Is this just about financial development, not stablecoins specifically?"** → Show effects persist conditional on aggregate financial development
4. **"Product complexity vs. Rajan-Zingales financial vulnerability"** → Run horse race; show complexity captures something beyond external finance dependence
5. **"Stablecoin data quality / geographic attribution"** → Use corridor-level payment cost proxies rather than on-chain flows for estimation; use adoption data for validation only
