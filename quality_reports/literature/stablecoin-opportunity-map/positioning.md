# Positioning Statement: Stablecoin Opportunity Map

**Project:** Stablecoin Opportunity Map -- Economic Complexity Meets Payment Innovation
**Stream:** Payment Infrastructure, Trade Costs, and Financial Frictions
**Date:** 2026-04-03

---

## 1. Suggested Contribution Statement

> We contribute to four literatures. First, we advance the emerging literature on payment infrastructure and trade (Ferrari Minesso, Lebastard & Triay Bagur, 2026) by introducing stablecoin adoption as a novel trade cost shifter in a structural gravity framework. Second, we contribute to the correspondent banking and de-risking literature (Rice, von Peter & Boar, 2020; Borchert, De Haas, Kirschenmann & Schultz, 2024) by identifying stablecoins as a potential alternative payment rail in corridors that have lost traditional banking access. Third, we extend the financial frictions and trade literature (Manova, 2013; Antras & Foley, 2015) by showing that payment frictions have heterogeneous effects across the product complexity distribution, with complex products -- which require more trade finance and longer payment cycles -- being disproportionately affected. Fourth, we connect the economic complexity literature (Hidalgo & Hausmann, 2009; Hausmann et al., 2014) to trade costs by demonstrating that a country's capacity to export complex products is constrained by its payment infrastructure.

## 2. Differentiation from Closest Papers

### vs. Auer, Lewrick & Paulick (2025) -- BIS WP 1265
**Their paper:** Gravity model for cross-border *crypto financial flows* (Bitcoin, Ether, USDT, USDC).
**Our paper:** Gravity model for bilateral *goods trade*, with stablecoin adoption as a trade cost modifier.
**Key difference:** They study where crypto flows *go*; we study how stablecoin infrastructure affects where *goods* go. We also introduce product-level heterogeneity via complexity, which they do not.

### vs. Ferrari Minesso, Lebastard & Triay Bagur (2026) -- ECB WP 3202
**Their paper:** Fast payment system interlinking increases bilateral trade by ~4%.
**Our paper:** Stablecoin adoption reduces payment frictions and increases trade, especially in complex products.
**Key difference:** They study government-led payment system interlinks; we study decentralized stablecoin adoption. They find an average effect; we estimate heterogeneous effects across the product complexity distribution.

### vs. Borchert, De Haas, Kirschenmann & Schultz (2024) -- CEPR DP 19373
**Their paper:** Loss of correspondent banking causes firm-level export declines in emerging Europe.
**Our paper:** In corridors where CBRs are lost, stablecoin adoption may partially substitute.
**Key difference:** They identify the *problem* (de-risking hurts trade); we identify a potential *solution* (stablecoin rails) and quantify where it would matter most (high-complexity-potential corridors).

### vs. Du, Huang & Scharfstein (2026) -- HBS WP
**Their paper:** Cost decomposition across three payment rails.
**Our paper:** Trade volume effects of payment rail availability.
**Key difference:** They compare costs; we estimate trade elasticities. Their analysis is descriptive; ours is causal (gravity with structural FEs).

### vs. Manova (2013) -- REStud
**Their paper:** Financial development x financial vulnerability => trade.
**Our paper:** Payment infrastructure x product complexity => trade.
**Key difference:** We focus specifically on payment/transaction frictions (not general financial development) and use economic complexity (not Rajan-Zingales external finance dependence) as the product-level moderator.

## 3. Anticipated Referee Concerns (from Literature)

| Concern | Source of Concern | Suggested Response |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| "Stablecoin adoption is endogenous to trade" | Standard gravity endogeneity (Ferrari Minesso et al. address this) | IV using regulatory shocks (crypto bans, CBDC launches); lagged adoption; de-risking severity as instrument |
| "Why not just measure remittance costs directly?" | Beck & Martinez Peria (2011) already do this | Remittance costs proxy for payment costs but miss the mechanism (stablecoin as alternative rail). We use both and show stablecoin adoption has effects beyond what remittance costs capture. |
| "Product complexity is just capital intensity" | Manova (2013) uses financial vulnerability | Show results are robust to controlling for Rajan-Zingales and persist for complexity conditional on financial dependence. |
| "On-chain data has geographic attribution problems" | Domain profile, referee concerns | Use Chainalysis adoption index + corridor-level remittance-to-stablecoin substitution data. Acknowledge limitations. |
| "Why would stablecoins matter for goods trade rather than just capital flows?" | Auer et al. (2025) focus on financial flows | Trade finance function: stablecoins facilitate B2B payments, reduce FX conversion layers, and accelerate settlement -- all of which reduce trade costs (Antras & Foley 2015, Niepmann & Schmidt-Eisenlohr 2017). |
