# Positioning Statement: Stablecoin Opportunity Map

**Date:** 2026-04-03
**Stream:** Stablecoins, Cryptocurrency, and Cross-Border Trade/Capital Flows

---

## Suggested Contribution Statement

> We contribute to three literatures. First, we extend the nascent literature on cross-border stablecoin flows (Auer, Lewrick & Paulick 2025; Reuter 2025; Cerutti, Obstfeld & Zhou 2024) by connecting stablecoin-driven payment cost reductions to product-level trade flows rather than aggregate bilateral crypto volumes. Second, we advance the economic complexity literature (Hidalgo et al. 2007; Hidalgo & Hausmann 2009) by introducing payment infrastructure as a binding constraint on countries' ability to access complex-product export markets. Third, we enrich the structural gravity literature on trade costs (Anderson & van Wincoop 2004; Santos Silva & Tenreyro 2006) by modeling stablecoin availability as a trade cost shifter that interacts heterogeneously with product complexity, generating predictions about which country x industry links are most likely to activate as payment frictions fall.

---

## Differentiation from Closest Papers

### vs. Auer, Lewrick & Paulick (2025) --- "DeFiying Gravity?"
- **They do:** Gravity model of bilateral crypto/stablecoin flows across 184 countries
- **They do not:** Connect these flows to goods trade, disaggregate by product, or incorporate economic complexity
- **We add:** Stablecoin availability enters our model as a payment friction variable in a goods-trade gravity, interacted with product complexity. We predict which trade links activate, not just which crypto flows exist.

### vs. Borchert, De Haas, Kirschenmann & Schultz (2024) --- "Broken Relationships"
- **They do:** Show that losing correspondent banking access reduces exports by 9.7pp
- **They do not:** Study how stablecoins could substitute for lost correspondent banking relationships, or which products are most affected
- **We add:** We study the mirror image: where stablecoin adoption could restore or create trade links that payment friction has blocked. We add the product complexity dimension to understand heterogeneous effects.

### vs. Cerutti, Firat & Perez-Saiz (2025) --- Digital Money Impact
- **They do:** Scenario analysis of 60% cost reduction from digital money on cross-border flows
- **They do not:** Use bilateral gravity estimation, disaggregate by product, or model stablecoin-specific channels
- **We add:** We estimate rather than assume the cost reduction; we use PPML gravity rather than scenario analysis; we disaggregate by product complexity.

### vs. Murakami & Viswanath-Natraj (2025) --- Stablecoin Solution for EMs
- **They do:** Theoretical model of stablecoin adoption driven by sovereign risk; empirical test of adoption determinants
- **They do not:** Study trade flows or connect to product-level export capacity
- **We add:** We take their insight that stablecoin demand is endogenous to macro conditions and ask the downstream question: conditional on adoption, which trade links benefit most?

### vs. Liu & Chen (2025) --- FinTech and Global Trade Gravity
- **They do:** Structural gravity with broad fintech index as trade cost shifter
- **They do not:** Focus on stablecoins specifically, disaggregate by product, or incorporate economic complexity
- **We add:** We use stablecoin-specific payment friction proxies (not a broad fintech index) and interact with product complexity, generating targeted predictions.

## Suggested Framing for Introduction

The introduction should follow this logic:

1. **Correspondent banking is retreating** (Rice et al. 2020; Borchert et al. 2024), raising payment costs especially for developing countries and creating "payment deserts" for international trade.

2. **Stablecoins have grown explosively** as cross-border payment instruments (Auer et al. 2025; Reuter 2025; BIS Bulletin 108), but their impact on real trade flows is unmeasured.

3. **Economic complexity theory** tells us that the ability to export complex products depends on accumulated capabilities (Hidalgo et al. 2007, 2009) -- but also implicitly on the ability to transact internationally. Payment frictions are a binding constraint that complexity theory has ignored.

4. **We ask:** Which country x industry links stand to benefit most from stablecoin-enabled payment cost reductions? We answer by building a "Stablecoin Opportunity Map" that combines structural gravity estimation with economic complexity metrics.
